## Art briefs

- **Backdrop:** Esagila temple storehouse beside a canal, around 1750 BCE; sun-warmed mud brick, grain jars, date palms, and an uncluttered dark foreground for dialogue. Avoid Egyptian pyramids, Roman columns, medieval castles, and text.
- **Mentor:** Babylonian temple scribe in a record room, pressing clay with a simple pale, cut reed stylus. The initial candidate was rejected because it depicted a modern-looking pencil/pen; the final asset was regenerated specifically to correct this anachronism.
- **Tableau:** Three sealed grain vessels, clay tablets, barley, balance stones, and a pale hollow reed cut on the diagonal to make a wedge-shaped stylus on a woven mat. Warm light comes from a small open oil lamp with a wick—not a candle. Avoid parchment, fountain pens, pencils, quills, metal nibs, inkwells, Egyptian hieroglyphics, and legible text.

## Historical correction — 2026-08-01

The original reconciliation tableau was replaced after review found a modern-looking pen/pencil and a candle. The revised image uses a cut-reed stylus, appropriate for pressing cuneiform into clay, and an open oil lamp. The historical basis is the [British Museum’s cuneiform-writing guide](https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/how-write-cuneiform) and its [Akkadian shell lamp](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1928-1009-406), dated 2300–2100 BCE and described as holding oil with a projecting wick.

Prompts used Codex built-in image generation on 2026-08-01. Final JPEG compression and sizing were performed locally; each asset remains below the 200 KiB per-image budget.
